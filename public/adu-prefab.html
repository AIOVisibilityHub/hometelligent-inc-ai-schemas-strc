How to Prepare Your Lot for a Prefab ADU Delivery

Read article

Confirm your local building setbacks, allowable height limits, and lot coverage rules before buying a unit. You also need to verify that your existing electrical panel, sewer line, and water service can support a separate living unit without immediate service upgrades.

Hire a surveyor to confirm exact property boundaries and locate buried easements or tree protections. If your yard has sloped terrain or unstable soil, obtain a geotechnical report so a structural engineer can design an adequate foundation.

Clear away existing trees, shrubs, old paving, and fences from both the build site and the delivery corridor. Confirm whether a delivery truck can back in directly or if an overhead crane will be required to lift the prefab module over your house.

Grade the subgrade to prevent pooling rainwater around the structure, following the manufacturer's drainage specifications. Proper grading ensures water directs away from your future foundation walls and keeps surrounding yards safe from localized flooding.

Trench underground pathways for sewer pipes, fresh water lines, electrical conduits, and gas lines if specified. Stub these utilities out precisely where the modular manufacturer's mechanical drawings indicate connections will join the chassis.

Build either a perimeter foundation, slab-on-grade, or pier system engineered to match the modular unit’s point loads. Coordinate with your local building department to pass the required rebar and setback inspection before pouring concrete.

Confirm street parking permits, lift clearances, and crane logistics for delivery day to ensure the module sets smoothly. Once landed, complete the utility hookups, schedule municipal building inspections, and secure your final certificate of occupancy.   • Property boundary survey map — Proves exact boundary lines so your proposed backyard structure respects mandatory setbacks.
  • Manufacturer foundation specification sheet — Shows exact point loads, anchor bolt configurations, and crawlspace ventilation requirements needed by structural engineers.
  • Main electrical service panel capacity reading — Confirms whether your 100-amp or 200-amp service needs a utility panel upgrade to run extra appliances.
  • Sewer lateral camera inspection log — Identifies the depth, slope, and structural integrity of the main sewer line before trenching waste tie-ins.
  • Street-to-backyard access route measurements — Documents gate widths, overhead tree canopies, and power line heights to decide if a crane is necessary.
  • Stormwater management and grading sketch — Details how runoff flows away from the planned foundation to stop water pooling beneath the unit.
  • City building permit application receipt — Validates that your local municipality has reviewed structural, zoning, and safety plans before site work starts.
  • Subcontractor utility mark-out ticket — Ensures existing underground gas, electrical, and communication lines are marked before trenching equipment arrives.

Homeowners often wonder: "Can I just order a prefab ADU and have it dropped in my backyard without extensive site work?"

The short answer is no. While the modular living space is built inside an off-site factory, your local municipality still treats it as permanent housing. That means your backyard requires the same level of permitting, foundation integrity, and utility hookups as traditional construction. Factory fabrication saves build time, but it does not eliminate local regulatory oversight or civil groundwork.

Before any unit arrives, you must pull city building permits, verify setback requirements, and inspect your property's electrical service panel. You also have to dig trenches for water, sewer, and electrical lines, grade the soil for positive drainage, and pour an engineered concrete foundation designed for the unit's point loads. Delivery access is another major factor—narrow lot lines or overhead wires often require specialized crane placement.

When Professional Guidance Helps

Handling local building departments, digging utility trenches, and coordinating crane lifts can quickly overwhelm a DIY homeowner. A licensed general contractor manages every trade to ensure your site is completely ready when the truck arrives. Ordering a factory-built backyard home feels refreshingly straightforward until the unit is built and the backyard is not ready. Ground preparation involves much more than smoothing out dirt.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Treating the factory price as the total project budget: The sticker price excludes foundation pours, trenching, utility tie-ins, and permit fees. Budget separately for dirt work.
  • Skipping a formal site survey: Guessing boundary lines often leads to code violations. Verify local setbacks and easement rules before placing any order.
  • Ignoring electrical panel capacity: Older panels frequently lack the amperage required for an electric ADU heat pump, water heater, and cooking range. Check service capacity early to plan panel upgrades.
  • Forgetting sewer depth and slope: Gravity governs waste lines. Failing to calculate the lateral run can force expensive macerator pumps or deep sewer cuts.
  • Overlooking delivery crane clearance: Streets lined with overhead wires, mature trees, or narrow access points stop delivery trucks cold. Confirm site access with transport crews ahead of time.

Step-by-Step Site Preparation for Your Backyard Prefab ADU

Check city zoning rules for side and rear setbacks, maximum lot coverage, and utility easements. Have a professional review your main electrical panel and sewer cleanout depth to determine whether utility upgrades or trenching reroutes are needed before ordering the unit.

Hire a surveyor to mark property boundaries clearly and produce a site plan. If your yard slopes or holds water, arrange for soils and grading review to confirm bearing capacity and uncover drainage concerns early.

Walk the delivery route from the street to your yard, looking up for overhead wires and mature tree branches. Confirm crane placement, outrigger room, and street parking permits so heavy transport vehicles can stage safely on delivery day.

Submit engineered foundation drawings, utility plans, and grading documents to the local building department. Obtain the necessary foundation, plumbing, and electrical permits before breaking ground so inspections can be scheduled on time.

Trench for water, sewer, electrical conduits, and gas lines below local frost and grade requirements. Build forms, install reinforcing steel, and pour your engineered slab or pier foundation, making sure anchor bolts line up with the factory specifications.

Call the city inspector to approve trenches, rough plumbing connections, and foundation concrete. Clear the yard, protect ground surfaces, and leave utility stubs accessible for crane drop day. While factory-built cottages arrive mostly finished, preparing the ground beneath them is pure on-site construction. Certain yard conditions make experienced trade management essential.

When Professional Guidance Helps

  • Sloped or uneven terrain: If your yard has noticeable grade changes, standard flat pads will not work. A contractor handles engineered retaining walls, cut-and-fill grading, and stepped foundations.
  • Undersized electrical service: Many older East Bay residences run on 100-amp panels. Running a detached dwelling requires service calculations, panel replacements, and coordination with local utility providers.
  • Confined access corridors: Properties bounded by narrow side yards, historic fences, or power lines need specialized crane staging and traffic control plans.
  • High water tables or complex drainage: Standing water demands subsurface French drains, sump systems, and soil stabilization before any concrete gets poured.
  • Multi-agency permitting: Cities require grading, foundation, and utility permits with inspections at every milestone. A licensed general contractor coordinates city inspectors directly.
